# Solus City Lore Bible

## Premise
Solus City is the last profitable port on a fractured coast where old nation-states collapsed into corporate feuds, syndicate wars, and black-market governance. In this city, identity is trust, trust is currency, and survival belongs to those who can prove both.

Every resident has two ledgers:
- an official profile the city pretends to recognize
- a shadow profile tracked by crews, fixers, and bounty brokers

In gameplay terms, your wallet signature is your identity, your RP is your reputation capital, and your syndicate is your insurance policy.

## Core Fantasy
You are not a hero. You are a contender.

You arrive unknown, shielded only by temporary newcomer protections while old powers measure your value. You build influence by committing crimes, training your body, arming your operation, and winning fights that people remember.

Your long-term goal is to become untouchable in a city that never forgets weakness.

## Era Timeline

### Year 0 - The Blackout
A cascading infrastructure failure took down regional grids, comms backbones, and banking rails across three neighboring territories. Solus survived because its port microgrid and private security contracts stayed online.

### Year 2 - The Corporate Evacuation
Megacorps abandoned full governance but left behind secured warehouses, debt records, and autonomous enforcement systems. Ex-employees and contractors formed the first proto-syndicates.

### Year 4 - The Reputation Wars
Without stable courts, disputes were settled by public outcomes: raids, duels, and mercenary contracts. Informal ranking circles became the de facto law.

### Year 6 - The Ledger Pact
Broker houses agreed on one principle: identity claims must be cryptographically provable. Wallet-signature authentication became the city standard. This is the beginning of modern Solus.

### Present Day
Syndicates compete for district influence, logistics chokepoints, and labor pools. The city runs on violence, optics, and profit margins.

## City Districts

### 1. The Docks
Salt, rust, and container labyrinths. Best place to disappear or ambush. Most entry-level jobs and low-tier crime contracts originate here.

Gameplay tie-in:
- Early crimes and low-RP target population
- Starter unit recruitment channels

### 2. Neon Mile
Entertainment corridor by day, predatory market by night. Casinos, VR dens, and data laundries hide higher-value opportunities behind social risk.

Gameplay tie-in:
- Mid-tier cash crime opportunities
- Reputation-sensitive confrontations

### 3. Iron Ward
Old industrial district converted into training pits, chop shops, and illegal fabrication labs. Combat crews and enforcers dominate.

Gameplay tie-in:
- Gym culture and stat obsession
- Equipment-heavy combat builds

### 4. Glass Heights
Corporate towers now run by boardless cartels and private family offices. Guards are expensive, grudges are longer, and losses are public.

Gameplay tie-in:
- High-RP targets
- Endgame syndicate influence

### 5. The Underspine
Service tunnels, storm drains, and data conduits beneath the city. Smugglers and recovery crews move what official routes cannot.

Gameplay tie-in:
- Rare syndicate operations and covert movement
- Story hooks for future seasonal content

## Major Factions

### Harbor Union
A labor syndicate turned protection machine. Controls loading crews and chokepoint access in The Docks.

Style:
- Defensive, territorial, attrition-based

### Violet Circuit
Information brokers and social engineers in Neon Mile. They weaponize rumor, visibility, and market timing.

Style:
- Opportunistic, high-risk, high-reward

### Ash Battalion
Former private security officers and deserters headquartered in Iron Ward.

Style:
- Discipline, drills, direct force

### Meridian Office
A quiet network of accountants, lawyers, and procurement brokers in Glass Heights.

Style:
- Long games, debt leverage, clean deniability

### Free Runners
Courier clans controlling the Underspine routes.

Style:
- Mobility, evasion, ambush contracts

## Social Order Of Solus
There are only four rules everyone accepts:
1. Signature proves identity.
2. Reputation outranks claims.
3. Debts are enforced.
4. Public losses have consequences.

Hospitalization, cooldowns, and revenge culture are not accidents of design; they are cultural norms in Solus. If you lose a fight, people expect retaliation. If you disappear too long, your rank decays in value even if your numbers do not.

## Key Characters (Season One Canon)

### Nyra Voss - The Notary Of Blood
Role: Independent verifier and broker of high-stakes disputes.

Nyra maintains the oldest surviving duel archive in Solus. If she records your win, it counts. If she records your collapse, everyone knows.

Use in game narrative:
- Introduces ranking significance and public consequence

### Calder Rook - Iron Ward Instructor
Role: Gym overseer and former assault commander.

Rook trains crews for syndicate contracts. He sees stats as language: strength is intent, defense is patience, speed is initiative, dexterity is survival.

Use in game narrative:
- Explains training progression and combat philosophy

### Mirel "Ledger" Anik
Role: Criminal accountant and logistics strategist.

Ledger maps risk to reward and sells route intelligence to syndicates. She claims every empire in Solus fails from poor cash discipline, not bad fighters.

Use in game narrative:
- Bridges crimes, economy loops, and shop progression

### Kade Thorn - Ghost Runner
Role: High-profile target specialist.

Kade rose from courier work to independent hunter. Famous for hitting stronger opponents and vanishing before revenge windows close.

Use in game narrative:
- Embodies high-RP risk and revenge loop

## Syndicates In Lore
Syndicates are not gangs; they are governance substitutes.

A syndicate provides:
- protection against random predation
- shared intelligence on targets and cooldown windows
- pooled reputation gravity (social deterrence)
- economic coordination

Why AP buffs make lore sense:
Coordinated teams fight better. Better logistics, better prep, better outcomes. The buff represents tactical uplift, not magical power.

## Meaning Of Reputation (RP)
RP is not morality. RP is market confidence in your threat profile.

High RP means:
- people believe you can enforce outcomes
- your wins are worth more attention
- your losses are worth more headlines

Low RP means:
- you are either new, inactive, or publicly broken

This is why beating higher-RP opponents matters more and why losses carry social cost.

## Newcomer Shield (Lore Justification)
New arrivals receive a temporary non-interference marker under the Ledger Pact. Veteran crews can observe but not engage directly until the marker expires.

The city justifies this as anti-chaos policy. In practice, it creates a controlled funnel of future talent into the conflict economy.

## NPC Archetypes
NPC opponents represent local power layers, not random mobs.

Examples:
- Street Thug: freelance violence economy
- Gang Lookout: territorial surveillance class
- Black Market Guard: private asset security
- Syndicate Runner: courier-combat hybrid
- Corporate Watchman: legacy corporate defense doctrine

As players climb, they are no longer fighting people. They are fighting institutions in human form.

## Themes
- Power is performed before it is possessed.
- Systems replace laws when trust collapses.
- Every win creates a debt someone wants repaid.
- Survival is short-term. Dominance is logistics.
